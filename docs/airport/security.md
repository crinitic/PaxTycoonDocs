# Security
With a [security manager](../personnel/jobs/security-manager.md), your airline can play dirty tricks on its rivals: a stink bomb in their lounge, geese in an engine, a fake payroll in their staff cafeteria. Their security manager stands between your trick and its target, and yours stands between theirs and you.

An attack is meant to be an annoyance, never a disaster. It costs its victim a little reputation, one aircraft for one day, or a few staff, and never cripples a fleet or bankrupts anybody.

Manage it from **Airport > Security**.

## What you need
To attack, you need the **Security manager** node from the [development tree](../development.md) and a security manager at work. Someone off sick or on a course does not count. In a game set up with **Everything unlocked**, you only need the manager.

You can attack any other airline in your game, computer or human, as long as it is still open.

## One a night
You can plan **one attack at a time**, because your manager is one person. Pick the target and the trick under **Plan an attack**. The attack happens that night, when you close the day. Until then you can **call it off**, for free.

The morning report tells you how it went, and the Security screen keeps a log of the last three months: what you did, and what was done to you.

## The attacks

| Attack | What it does | Share of your takings | At least |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stink bomb | Something unspeakable goes off in their home lounge | 3% | ¤200,000 |
| Rubber chicken | One of their passengers is served a rubber chicken | 3% | ¤200,000 |
| Boarding call | For a day, their boarding calls end by saying you are better | 3% | ¤200,000 |
| Free champagne | A fake press release promises free champagne on every flight | 3% | ¤200,000 |
| Snakes on a flight | Snakes on one of their flights | 3% | ¤200,000 |
| Geese in the engine | One of their aircraft cannot fly for a day | 6% | ¤400,000 |
| Sugar in the tank | Some of their stored fuel is ruined | 5% | ¤350,000 |
| Headhunting raid | Some of their staff come over to you | 8% | ¤500,000 |
| Cafeteria payroll | A fake, inflated payroll upsets some of their staff | 5% | ¤350,000 |

A stink bomb needs a lounge at their home airport. Against an airline without one, it is not offered.

### Reputation attacks
The first five cost the victim [reputation](../reputation.md). Each one counts as an incident against them, and wears off over 12 weeks as incidents do. How bad it is depends on your manager: a Disaster or Questionable one makes it **Minor**, a Competent or Impressive one **Moderate**, and an Exceptional one **Major**. Against an airline that flew fewer than 1,000 flights in the last 12 weeks, it is never worse than Moderate.

### Geese in the engine
One of their aircraft does not fly for a day: the day the victim reads about it in the morning report. All its flights that day are cancelled. They have the day to see it coming, but not to fix it, since the day's flights are already locked. A good manager picks their busiest aircraft; a poor one picks at random. The geese prefer an aircraft that ends its day where it started, so nothing else is out of place the day after.

The victim loses that day's takings, and nothing else: these cancellations do not count against their reputation.

### Sugar in the tank
Ruins some of the fuel in their [tanks](../hq/fuel.md#tanks): up to 5% of it, with an Exceptional manager. An airline with empty tanks loses nothing.

### Headhunting raid
Some of their pilots, flight attendants, gate agents, mechanics and lounge staff come over to you, about 3% of them with a top manager, and always at least one. Specialists never do, and neither does anyone off sick or on a course. Nobody knows beforehand who or how good they will be.

They join you that day, on the pay they had, with no hiring fee. They arrive on the bench, not on an aircraft. If a pilot or flight attendant leaves an aircraft short, it cannot fly until the gap is filled. A raid takes at most one aircraft's worth of crew.

Someone headhunted does not count against the victim's [turnover](../reputation.md#staff-30-points). Nobody quit on them.

### Cafeteria payroll
Some of their employees see a payroll they were not meant to see, specialists included: up to 30% of their staff with a top manager. Each of them loses some satisfaction. It happens after that night's resignations, so the victim has a day to answer it with [raises](../personnel/salary-increases.md) before anyone walks out.

## Foiled or landed
Whether an attack works is down to the two security managers. Only each side's best one at work counts.

- **Equal managers**: about 1 attack in 5 lands.
- **A better defender**: most attacks fail, and the bigger the gap the more, but some always get through.
- **A weaker defender**: most attacks land. Against a Disaster defender, an Exceptional attacker almost always gets through, but never always.
- **No manager at all**: everything lands, unless you have attacked lately.

**The more you attack, the less it works.** Every attack you made in the last 28 days, landed or foiled, against anyone, makes the next one a little more likely to fail. Even against an airline with nobody in the job: the airport police notice someone who keeps doing it.

How much a landed attack does is down to your manager alone. The defender's manager never softens a hit that lands.

## What each side learns
You learn whether your attack landed. You do not learn how much it did, except for the people a headhunting raid brought you.

The victim always learns what happened, or what was tried. If they have a security manager, they also learn **who** did it. Without one, the attacker is an unknown airline, even on the boarding calls.

Attacks never make the [News](../intel/news.md). Only the two airlines know.

## On alert
A landed attack puts its target **on alert** against that kind of attack, from anyone, for 28 days. A foiled one does not. The plan screen says when a target is still on alert after the last one, so you know something landed on them recently.

If two airlines plan the same attack on the same target on the same night, only the first to land goes ahead. The second is called off, and costs nothing.

## Fees
Every attack has a fee, quoted when you plan it:

- a **share of what your airline took in the last 28 days**, by kind, as in the table above, and never less than its minimum;
- **more for a better manager**, because a better manager hits harder: the best costs up to three times what the worst does;
- **25% more for every attack you made in the last 28 days**, the same count that makes it harder to land.

The fee depends on your size, not the target's, so a rich airline cannot spam cheap attacks, and a small one is not priced out of stinging the leader.

The fee is charged that night, **whether the attack lands or is foiled**, and is not refunded. You need the money in the bank to plan it, and again at night: if your balance cannot cover it then, the attack is called off and costs nothing. Calling it off yourself costs nothing either.

The night charges what the fee works out to then, which is close to the quote, since only that day's flying can change it.

## Computer airlines
Every [computer airline](../competitors.md) has a security manager, so they defend from the start. They also attack, by the same rules and for the same fees, and never with money they need to keep flying. They only start a fight with a player who holds the **Security manager** node, so a new airline gets a quiet start.

How often depends on their kind: Opportunist the most, then Budget, then Regional. Legacy never starts a fight. They go for the player who took the most in the last 28 days.

**Attack one and it may answer**, whatever its kind, Legacy included, and whether or not you hold the node, if its manager caught who did it. It decides once, at its next review: more often than not, it answers.

Computer airlines never attack each other.

## A game setting
Sabotage is a game setting. When you set up a new game, untick **Sabotage** to play without it. It is on by default, and cannot be changed once the game has started. With sabotage off, nobody can attack, and the Security tile says so.

## In the ledger
A fee is a **Sabotage** line under **The night**, named after the target. The morning report lists it with the night's other bills.

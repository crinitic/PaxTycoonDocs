# Absence and Incidents
## Sickness
Every night, anyone who worked that day might fall ill. Someone who falls ill misses the next day at least, and is usually off for about a week. They are still paid while they are off, and their satisfaction keeps falling as it does at work.

Sickness is not something to manage day by day. The answer to it is a few spare staff, kept on the bench.

### Who falls ill
Only people who worked that day can fall ill, the same people who can cause an incident: pilots and flight attendants on an aircraft that carried passengers, lounge staff when a lounge was open, and every gate agent and mechanic. Specialists and trainers never fall ill. Neither does anyone on the bench, in training, or already off sick.

Nobody falls ill during your game's first month, its first 28 days. After that, sickness is part of every game, and cannot be turned off.

### How often
A crew you keep reasonably content has about 1 or 2 people in every 100 off sick on an average day. Unhappy staff fall ill a little more often, and well-paid, Happy staff a little less. **Miserable** staff fall ill about three times as often again: a Miserable crew can have 6 or 7 in every 100 off at once.

Raises are how you keep it down. See [Satisfaction](salary-increases.md).

### Who takes their place
You never re-roster by hand. A pilot or flight attendant who falls ill comes off their aircraft, and a spare from the bench takes their place, as when someone quits. When they are better, they come back to the bench, or straight onto an aircraft that is short.

If there is no spare, the aircraft is a crew member short and its flights are cancelled until somebody is back or you hire. A gate agent off sick with no one to cover leaves a gate idle, lounge staff off sick can keep a lounge closed, and a mechanic off sick tends no aircraft.

**Keep about 5% spare crew**: one spare pilot for every 20 you need, one spare flight attendant for every 20 you need, and never fewer than one of each. That covers almost every day, whatever the size of your airline. Miserable staff fall ill too often for 5% to be enough.

### On your screens
Anyone off sick still counts in your headcount, since you still pay them. But the spare or short count beside it only counts the people at work. A line such as **12 pilots · 3 off sick · 1 short** means you employ 12 pilots, 3 of them are off, and the 9 at work are 1 fewer than your fleet needs.

The morning report says how many people fell ill overnight, without naming them. If spares covered everyone, it is a line under the other updates. If an aircraft is left short, it is an urgent box that names every aircraft short of crew. When someone who was off sick is back and an aircraft is fully crewed again, the morning report says that too.

## Incidents
Every night, anyone who worked that day might cause an incident. Most are embarrassing, some are expensive. A pilot tries a loop for fun. A gate agent lets a goat on as carry-on. A flight attendant serves coffee with salt instead of sugar. A mechanic refills the coffee machine with hydraulic fluid.

Nothing is delayed, diverted or grounded by an incident: your aircraft always keep to their schedule. The damage is to your money and your name.

### Who causes them
Pilots, flight attendants, gate agents, lounge staff and mechanics can all cause incidents. Specialists and trainers never do.

Only people who worked that day can cause one. A pilot or flight attendant has to have been on an aircraft that carried passengers, and lounge staff have to have had a lounge open. Anyone who is sick, in training, or who quit that night was not at work.

What makes an incident likely is **low competence** or **low satisfaction**. Some incidents come from not knowing the job, some from not caring about it, and a few need both. The lower someone sits on either scale, the more likely they are to cause one, and it climbs steeply at the bottom.

Good staff almost never cause incidents. A starting airline of new hires will rarely see one. A roster of Disaster or Miserable employees will see them most nights. Incidents come from bad hires and missed raises, not from how big your airline is. Training and raises are how you prevent them. See [Training](training.md) and [Satisfaction](salary-increases.md).

### How bad they are
Every incident is **Minor**, **Moderate** or **Major**, and the same incident is always the same severity: a pilot who tries a loop for fun is never a Minor incident. Severity decides how much an incident costs your reputation, and how much it costs if it pays compensation.

| Severity | Examples | Reputation | Compensation, if any |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor | salty coffee, a party in the lounge, a fake fee at the gate | counts once | about a quarter of a flight's takings |
| Moderate | a passenger sent to the wrong flight, a passenger locked in the lavatory | counts 3 times | about a whole flight's takings |
| Major | a loop for fun, a goat on board, a fight on board | counts 10 times, and makes the news | about three flights' takings |

### What they cost
Every incident is kept on record against your airline, and counts against its reputation, more the worse it was. See [Reputation](../reputation.md).

Some incidents also cost **compensation**: the ones where a passenger lost something real, like their flight, their luggage or their money. Rudeness and bad service cost reputation only, however bad they were. A Major incident can make the news without costing you anything in compensation.

How much you pay depends on three things:

- **how bad it was**, its severity;
- **what your flights earned that day**, so long routes and big aircraft pay more for the same mistake;
- **how big your airline is.** A bigger airline pays more for the same incident, but less than in proportion: an airline flying 100 flights a day pays about 4½ times what one flying 5 pays, while earning 20 times as much. Up to 5 flights a day, size makes no difference.

A [Lawyer](jobs/lawyer.md) reduces compensation. They do not make incidents any less likely, and they do not keep one out of the news.

An incident's page in HQ shows what the passengers asked for and what you paid, so you can see what your lawyer saved you.

### In the news
Every Major incident makes the [News](../intel/news.md), which every airline in your game can read. The story names your airline and the job, never the employee. It stays in the news for 12 weeks, as long as it counts against your reputation.

### In the morning report
The morning report names everyone who caused an incident overnight, and what they did. A Major incident says it made the news. An employee's page shows how many incidents they have caused in total.

### In HQ
**Incidents**, in the HQ app, lists every incident your airline has had, newest first, each tagged with its severity. A Major one still in the news says so. You can filter the list by severity or by cause.

Tap an incident to see what happened: who caused it, how bad it was, whether it made the news, and why it happened, low competence, low satisfaction or both. If it cost compensation, the page shows what the passengers asked for and what you paid after your lawyer. Tap the employee's name to open their page. A former employee's name opens nothing.

Previous and next walk the list as you filtered it.

### In the ledger
Compensation is paid every night for all of that night's incidents together, as one **Compensation** line under **The night**.

### Turning incidents off
Incidents are a game setting. When you set up a new game, untick **Staff incidents** to play without them. It is on by default, and cannot be changed once the game has started. With incidents off, every airline earns the full incident points of its reputation.

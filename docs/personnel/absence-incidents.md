## Incidents
Every night, anyone who worked that day might cause an incident. Most are embarrassing, some are expensive. A pilot tries a loop for fun. A gate agent lets a goat on as carry-on. A flight attendant serves coffee with salt instead of sugar. A mechanic refills the coffee machine with hydraulic fluid.

Nothing is delayed, diverted or grounded by an incident: your aircraft always keep to their schedule. The damage is to your money and your name.

### Who causes them
Pilots, flight attendants, gate agents, lounge staff and mechanics can all cause incidents. Specialists and trainers never do.

Only people who worked that day can cause one. A pilot or flight attendant has to have been on an aircraft that carried passengers, and lounge staff have to have had a lounge open. Anyone who is sick, in training, or who quit that night was not at work.

What makes an incident likely is **low competence** or **low satisfaction**. Some incidents come from not knowing the job, some from not caring about it, and a few need both. The lower someone sits on either scale, the more likely they are to cause one, and it climbs steeply at the bottom.

Good staff almost never cause incidents. A starting airline of new hires will rarely see one. A roster of Disaster or Miserable employees will see them most nights. Incidents come from bad hires and missed raises, not from how big your airline is. Training and raises are how you prevent them. See [Training](training.md) and [Satisfaction](salary-increases.md).

### What they cost
Every incident is kept on record against your airline, and will count against its reputation.

Some incidents also cost **compensation**: the ones where a passenger lost something real, like their flight, their luggage or their money. Rudeness and bad service cost reputation only. How much you pay depends on how bad it was and on what your flights earned that day, so a big airline flying long routes pays more for the same mistake.

A [Lawyer](jobs/lawyer.md) reduces compensation. They do not make incidents any less likely.

### In the morning report
The morning report names everyone who caused an incident overnight, and what they did. An employee's page shows how many incidents they have caused in total.

### In the ledger
Compensation is paid every night for all of that night's incidents together, as one **Compensation** line under **The night**.

### Turning incidents off
Incidents are a game setting. When you set up a new game, untick **Staff incidents** to play without them. It is on by default, and cannot be changed once the game has started.

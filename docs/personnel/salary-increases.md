# Satisfaction
Every employee has a satisfaction, shown on their page as one of five labels:

- Miserable
- Unhappy
- Okay
- Happy
- Delighted

A new hire starts out Happy. From then on, their satisfaction falls a little every night, whatever you do. Some people sour faster than others. Left alone, a new hire turns Unhappy after about a year.

## Raises
A raise is the only thing that lifts satisfaction. It puts 10% on the person's pay, and that is the only size there is.

Raise someone from their page under **Personnel**, with the **Raise** button next to **Fire**. The dialog shows their pay now, the increase and their pay after the raise.

A raise costs nothing on the day. The nightly payroll charges the new pay from that night on, so there is no line for it in the ledger, only bigger **Wages**. Their page shows the new pay, and **Last raise** shows today.

Every raise helps by the same amount, however each raise is 10% of pay that already includes every earlier raise, so someone you have kept for years is paid well above what they signed for, and well above a new hire in the same job.

You can raise anyone you employ, including people who are sick or in training, and people who are already Delighted. A raise for someone who is already content buys very little, and that is your call.

## Raising everyone
The Personnel hub has a **Raise everyone** button under the payroll. It gives every employee a 10% raise at once. The dialog shows how many people that is, the payroll now, the increase and the payroll after.

Raise everyone is convenient, but it is the expensive way to do it. It raises people who are still Happy or Delighted along with the ones who needed it, and that extra pay is charged every night from then on. Raising one person at a time, when they turn Unhappy, keeps the same staff content for less.

## Quitting
Miserable employees may quit. Every night, each Miserable employee has a small chance of walking out, and the chance grows the lower they sink. Nobody quits while they are only Unhappy, so Unhappy is your cue to act.

Someone who quits is gone before that night's flights: they are not paid for the night, and they do not work it. The morning report names everyone who quit.

If a pilot or flight attendant who quit was on an aircraft, a spare one from the bench takes their place, as when you fire someone. If you have no spare, the aircraft is a crew member short and its flights are cancelled. The next pilot or flight attendant you hire goes straight onto it.

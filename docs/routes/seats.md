# Seats
The **Seats** section of a route's screen shows how many seats are flying the route on a given day: yours, your [rivals'](../competitors.md), and, with a route advisor, how they compare with the passengers who want to fly it. It sits under [catering](catering.md).

It only shows. To change what flies, change your schedule.

## Where it shows
- On a route you hold, rented or seasonal, it is always there.
- On any other route, it is there only if you have an [informer](../personnel/jobs/informer.md), and then it shows rivals only.

A route you don't hold, with no informer, has no Seats section.

## Choosing a day
It shows one day at a time, from today to 7 days ahead. Step through the days with **‹ ›**. It opens on today, and the line under the date says how far off the day is: **Today**, **In 1 day**, **In 7 days**.

It counts the flights already on your board for that day, so a one-off counts and a cancelled flight does not. Ferry flights never count. Days and departure times are in your home airport's time, as on your flight board.

## Each direction
Demand is quoted each way, so each direction is shown on its own. Pick it with **Departures from**.

## Your flights
**You** lists each of your departures that day: its time, the aircraft and its seats. Above them, a line adds up your flights, aircraft and seats.

If you fly nothing that day in that direction, it says so.

## Rivals
With an informer, every rival flying that day is listed by name. Tap a name to open everything your informer knows about it.

From a **Competent** informer, each rival also shows its flights and seats that day. Below that, rivals are named with nothing beside them.

If your informer knows of nobody else flying that day, it says so.

## Through the day
From a **Questionable** route advisor, the section draws the day's hourly curve, the hours people want to leave, with your departures marked on it. With an informer, rivals' departures are marked too, in their own style.

## Seats against demand
From a **Competent** route advisor, a bar puts the day's seats against the day's passengers, all cabins together:

- **You**, your seats, in yellow.
- **Rivals**, their seats, in teal. This needs a Competent informer too; without one, the bar is yours alone.
- **Without a seat**, the passengers nobody has a seat for, in grey.

Under the bar, a line says what share of the passengers have a seat: **Seats for 54% of 1,863 passengers**.

When there are more seats than passengers, the bar is drawn to the seats instead, a **Demand** mark shows where the passengers run out, and the key adds **More than demand** with how many seats are left over. The share goes over 100%. The bar does not say whose seats will fly empty: that comes down to fares and departure times.

How exact the passengers are depends on your advisor, and the line under the bar says which it is:

- **Competent**: an average day in that month. It knows the season but not the week.
- **Impressive**: that date exactly, weekday included.
- **Exceptional**: that date at your fares. For each cabin, it uses the average fare across your flights that day in that direction, weighted by their seats, and names the fares it used. A cabin you don't sell has no fare of yours to price, so its passengers and any rival seats in it are left out of the bar, and a line says so. If you fly nothing that day in that direction, there is no bar at all.

None of these figures are guesses: each is exact for what it says it is. At your fares, it works demand out as the advisor's fare chart does, as if nobody else flew the route.

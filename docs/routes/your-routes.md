# Your routes
**Your routes** is where you run the routes you hold. Each one says how it did over the last 7 days, opens into a week of its departures, and is where you set its [fares](fares.md). It opens from its tile on the **Routes** screen.

## The list
There is one row per route you hold, rented or seasonal. A route held by both an offer and a rental is one row, with both on it.

Narrow the list to **Seasonal offers** or **Rentals**, and order it:

- **Ending soonest**: offers by their days left, with a rental being lost tonight first.
- **Nearest first**.
- **Lowest load first**: every cabin together. Routes with nothing flown go last.
- **Most priced out**.
- **Lowest profit**.

Routes you lost and cannot rent again yet are listed at the foot, with the day you can.

## A route's row
- **The route**, with an **Offer** or **Rented** badge and its distance.
- **Profit or loss** over the last 7 days, in green or red, with the word under it.
- **How much flies it**: weekly flights, both directions together, and the aircraft flying them.
- **The right it is held on**: an offer's days left, a rental's rent a month.
- **Load per cabin**: passengers over seats on the flights flown in the last 7 days, both directions together. Only cabins with seats on those flights are shown.
- **Signals**, counted in flights: **▼ 3 flights priced out** and **▲ 4 flights sold out**. A flight counts once for each mark any of its cabins got, so one whose Business sold out and whose Economy was priced out counts for both.

A route with nothing flown in the last 7 days says so instead, with the day of its next flight.

**Sold out** means a cabin filled and more people wanted to fly: add seats, or raise the fare. **Priced out** means a cabin flew with empty seats while people who wanted to travel did not buy: lower the fare, or improve what they get for it.

The last 7 days are the seven before today. Today has not flown yet.

### Keeping a rental
A rented route also says whether it is flying enough seats to keep it:

- **More than enough seats to keep the route**, in green.
- **Just enough seats to keep the route**, in amber.
- **Not enough seats to keep the route · lost tonight**, in red.
- **Not judged yet**, in grey, while it is still in its grace period.

Tap it for the seats scheduled over the last 28 days against the seats needed. More flights, or a bigger aircraft on the flights it has, add seats.

## The profit
A route's profit is its own, and nothing else's:

- **Takings**, from every flight that flew.
- **Fuel** and **catering**, already on each flight.
- **Rent** for the 7 days, on a rental.
- **Offer fee** for the 7 days, on an offer. The fee is paid once, when you take the offer, and is spread evenly over the offer's days here, so an offer's first week is not a loss it never made.

Wages, upkeep, gates, lounges and interest belong to the airline as a whole. No share of them is put onto a route, so a route in profit can still be one your airline does not earn from. The ledger has the whole airline.

## The open row
Tap a row to open it. One opens at a time, so opening another closes the first.

It starts with the 7 days broken down, both directions together: takings, fuel, catering, rent or the offer fee, and the profit or loss.

Below that, it shows one direction at a time. It opens on the one leaving your home airport, or else the busier one. Switch with the buttons at the top.

**Route fares** says what the direction's flights charge before their offsets, and **Change fares** sets them. See [Fares](fares.md).

### The week
The grid is the direction's weekly flights: departure times down the side and the weekdays across, on your home airport's clock. Each departure has a row per cabin, for the cabins any flight that way has seats in.

**% Load** shows how full each flight was over its last 4 runs:

- **▲** sold out, in green, and **▼** priced out, in amber, from the latest of those runs that had either.
- **new**: not flown yet.
- A hatched cell: that aircraft has no seats in the cabin. It is not 0%.
- A blank cell: nothing leaves then on that weekday.

**Fare** shows what each flight charges from tomorrow instead.

A flight with an [offset](fares.md#a-flights-offset) has it beside its time, **18:00 +10%**. Where one time's flights have different offsets, each cell shows its own.

When two of your aircraft leave on the same weekday at the same time, their cell adds them together.

Once-only flights are not in the grid. They are listed under it, with their date, time, aircraft and load.

**Route details ›** opens the route's own screen, for its [catering](catering.md), [seats](seats.md), terms and your route advisor.

### A flight's card
Tap a cell, or a once-only flight, to open its card. Where two aircraft share the cell, pick which one first.

- The day and time, and whether it flies every week or once.
- The aircraft, with its seat comfort and entertainment stars.
- Each of its last 4 runs: the date, and each cabin's passengers of its seats, with any mark.
- **Offset from the route fare**, with the fare it gives each cabin beside it. **Save** sets it from the flight's next flight, and the card says which day that is.
- **Edit flight**, for everything else about the flight.

With an **Exceptional** [route advisor](../personnel/jobs/route-advisor.md), the card also says what the fare does to each cabin's demand.

# Reputation
Your reputation is a score out of 100, shown next to your airline's name at the top of the screen. Click it to see what each part of your airline earned, and how you rank against the other airlines in the game.

## A score, and nothing more
Reputation does not change your demand, your fares or anything you pay. Passengers do not choose you for it. It is there for prestige.

The last few points need everything to be perfect, so there is always something left to chase once money is no longer a worry.

## When it changes
Your score is worked out again every night, from how your airline has run over the **last 12 weeks**. Recent weeks count more than old ones: yesterday counts in full, a day six weeks ago counts half, and a day twelve weeks ago barely counts at all. Busy days also count more than quiet ones. Turnover is the one exception: it looks at the **last 4 weeks** only, in the same way.

So a bad week does not vanish from your score one morning. It fades out over the following weeks, and a good week takes time to show.

A new airline has no score. Your first night gives you one, so it appears on the morning of your second day. Anything you have not done yet, such as flying, earns no points and reads **No data yet**, so expect a low first score that fills in as you fly.

## What counts
Points scale evenly between the two ends of each line, so halfway between them earns half the points.

### Flight (45 points)
These count every passenger you fly, so a full flight counts for more than an empty one.

| Factor | Points | Full points | No points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seats | 5 | three-star seats | one-star seats |
| Entertainment | 5 | three-star entertainment | one-star entertainment |
| Meals | 5 | Feast | Snack |
| Value for money | 10 | 25% or more under the route's fare, for what you offer | 25% or more over it |
| Aircraft condition | 10 | condition 90 or above | condition 40 |
| Lounges | 10 | a Palace where the passenger departs | no lounge |

**Value for money** is not about being cheap. Your fare is weighed against what the passenger gets for it, the same way passengers compare airlines: seats, entertainment, meals and the lounge. An airline that charges more for a better product loses nothing. One that charges the route's fare for one-star seats, one-star entertainment, a Snack and no lounge earns only half. With everything at its best, you can charge about 8% over the route's fare and still earn all 10 points.

**Aircraft condition** is the condition of the aircraft that actually flew, averaged over the passengers on board and the last 12 weeks. So it can differ from today's condition on the Aircraft screen, which counts every aircraft once, including those on the ground. Kept on the Standard maintenance program, a fleet earns 8 of the 10 points. Minimal earns 4, and Conservative earns all 10.

**Lounges** count at every airport you fly from, including seasonal and rented routes. A lounge that did not open because you were short of [lounge staff](personnel/jobs/lounge-staff.md) counts as no lounge. You can build a lounge at your home airport and at both ends of every route you rent, so if every airport you fly from is one of those, a Palace at each earns all 10 points. Seasonal routes between those airports are fine too. An airport you only reach with a seasonal route cannot have a lounge, so flights leaving from it earn nothing here. See [Lounges](airport/lounges.md).

### Staff (30 points)

| Factor | Points | Full points | No points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Satisfaction | 10 | Delighted, on average | Miserable, on average |
| Competence | 10 | Exceptional, on average | Disaster, on average |
| Turnover | 10 | nobody leaves | 5% of your staff leave at once |

Satisfaction counts everyone. Competence leaves specialists out. The dialog shows both as an average out of 100, which is the only place you see these as numbers.

**Turnover** is the share of your staff who left in the last 4 weeks. Someone who left yesterday counts in full and fades out over the 4 weeks, so if 1 person out of 100 left yesterday, the dialog reads 1%, and half that two weeks later.

**Turnover counts everyone who leaves, whether they quit or you fire them.** Firing costs no money, but it costs reputation just as quitting does. See [Satisfaction](personnel/salary-increases.md).

### Reliability (25 points)

| Factor | Points | Full points | No points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incidents | 15 | no incidents | 20 points or more for every 1,000 flights |
| Cancellations | 10 | no cancellations | 1 flight in 10 cancelled |

**Incidents are weighted by how bad they were.** A Minor incident counts 1 point, a Moderate one 3 and a Major one 10, so a single Major incident in 1,000 flights costs half the 15. Average staff will cause about 2 points' worth of incidents for every 1,000 flights. A small airline feels each one more, because it flies fewer flights for them to be spread over. See [How bad they are](personnel/absence-incidents.md#how-bad-they-are).

**Every cancellation counts**, whatever the reason: no crew, an aircraft that could not fly, an aircraft in the wrong place, or no gate. A passenger does not care why their flight did not go. Ferry flights carry nobody and count for nothing, here or anywhere else on this page.

If your game was set up with **Staff incidents** turned off, every airline earns the full 15 incident points. See [Incidents](personnel/absence-incidents.md#incidents).

## Other airlines
The dialog lists every other airline in your game with its score, in order, with yours among them. You see their scores, never their breakdowns. An airline that has no score yet is listed last.

## History
Once your airline has two nights of scores, the dialog draws a chart of your score on every night of the **last month**, **3 months** or **year**. Switch between them with **1M**, **3M** and **1Y** above the chart. It opens on 3 months, the same 12 weeks your score is worked out from. Early in a game, the chart shows every day since the game began and says so underneath.

Your line is the thick amber one, drawn over the others. The other airlines in your game are drawn beside it, each from its first scored night: a line that begins with an open dot is an airline that got its first score inside the range. The list of other airlines is the chart's key, with each airline's colour before its name. A colour stays with its airline, so overtaking someone never repaints their line.

### Following airlines
Every other airline has its own colour, and they are all coloured when the dialog opens. To clear the chart, tap an airline in the list to draw its line in thin grey under the others, and tap it again to give it its colour back. With more than five other airlines, colours are used again, so go by the list and the callout to tell those lines apart. Your picks are forgotten when you close the dialog.

### Picking a day
Tap or click a day on the chart to see everyone's score that night, highest first. Only the coloured airlines are named; the grey ones that had a score are counted, as in **and 6 others**. An airline that had no score yet that day is left out. Tap the same day again, or switch the range, to put it away.

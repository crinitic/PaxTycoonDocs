# Aircraft age
Every aircraft keeps count of the hours it has flown in its whole life, not only with you. Hours are what wear an aircraft out for good: an older aircraft wears faster, and at **120,000 hours** it is scrapped.

## Older aircraft wear faster
Every aircraft loses a little condition each day, and more for every hour it flies. How fast also depends on your [mechanics](../personnel/jobs/mechanic.md). On top of that, the older the airframe, the faster it wears:

| Hours | Wears |
|---:|---|
| 0 | as fast as a new aircraft |
| 18,000 | 1.02× as fast |
| 40,000 | 1.11× as fast |
| 70,000 | 1.34× as fast |
| 100,000 | 1.69× as fast |
| 120,000 | twice as fast |

It rises smoothly, hour by hour, with no step anywhere, so there is no point at which selling suddenly makes sense. Most of it comes late in an aircraft's life. It applies on the ground too: an old aircraft that is parked still wears faster than a new one that is parked.

Faster wear costs you through the things condition already affects: upkeep rises as condition falls, worn aircraft count against your [reputation](../reputation.md), they sell for less, and an aircraft worn too far is grounded. Age has no other penalty.

!!! warning "Minimal maintenance on an old aircraft"
    On **Minimal**, a young aircraft whose weekly maintenance happens can never be grounded between checks. Past about 108,000 hours, an aircraft flown round the clock with no mechanic looking after it can be.

## The life limit
Every aircraft, whatever its type, flies **120,000 hours** and no more. Nothing extends it.

The night an aircraft reaches it, it is scrapped. You do not have to do anything:

- You are paid **5% of its type's list price**, whatever its condition and cabin.
- Its crew move to aircraft that are short of people.
- Its flights after today come off the board.

The morning tells you which aircraft was scrapped and lists the flights it took with it, and the scrap shows in your statement as **Scrapped aircraft**. It also makes the [news](../intel/news.md).

Only flying adds hours. A parked aircraft never gets any closer to the limit.

## What an old aircraft is worth
An aircraft is worth less the more hours it has flown and the worse its condition. Close to the limit, its value fades towards the scrap price and meets it exactly at 120,000 hours, so selling an aircraft the day before it is scrapped gets you no more than letting it be scrapped.

The used market never sells aircraft with more than 70,000 hours.

## What you see
On an aircraft's **Spec** tab, under **Hours**:

- **Age**, a word for how old it is:

    | Hours | Age |
    |---:|---|
    | under 30,000 | Young |
    | 30,000 to 70,000 | Mature |
    | 70,000 to 100,000 | Old |
    | 100,000 and over | Near retirement |

- **Retirement**, the hours it has left before it is scrapped.

Every aircraft on the used market shows its age word after its hours.

How much faster an aircraft wears, and when it will reach the limit, is what an [aircraft advisor](../personnel/jobs/aircraft-advisor.md#aircraft-age) tells you.
